The prose is already at publication level. Only the points below would make me stumble
as a native-speaking psychologist reader. Everything not listed I would leave as it is.

A. ERRORS AND WRONG OR LOOSE WORDING
------------------------------------
1.  Abstract (p.1): "...it deterred delegation in two ways. At first contact, ... And
    second, categorical non-delegation..."
    -> "...in two ways. First, at first contact, ...; second, categorical non-delegation..."

2.  Acknowledgements footnote (p.1): the list is ungrammatical ("...the lively research
    group at the TWI, participants of numerous workshops and conferences").
    -> "We are grateful to Chris Starmer and Robin Cubitt, to whom we owe several
    aspects of the design, to the research group at the TWI, and to participants at
    numerous workshops and conferences. We also thank the editor and three anonymous
    reviewers."

3.  Hypothesis label (p.6): typeset "H 1." but cited everywhere as "H1".
    -> "H1." (the label comes from \newtheorem{hyp}{H}; remove the space).

4.  Sec. 5.2 (p.31): "our cumulative density functions" is the wrong term.
    -> "our empirical cumulative distribution functions".

5.  Sec. 3.1 (p.12): a sentence begins with a numeral ("240 participants completed...").
    -> "In total, 240 participants completed the study."

6.  Sec. 3.3 (p.15), first paragraph: tense and syntax drift ("being asked to indicate",
    "would read", "would have to give consent to ... our using of their data").
    -> plain simple past: "participants saw ... and were asked to indicate ... They then
    read ... and consented to the use of their data for this publication ...".
    Also: "At the time, the agentic AIs would typically choose the traffic light"
    -> "[At the time, ]Agentic AI tools typically answered 'traffic light', presumably because of the
    colors of the shapes."

7.  Sec. 4.6 (p.27): the run-in heading "Which reasons provided are most tightly linked
    to delegation?" does not match the text below it (expectations), or question (ii)
    on p.26 ("Which expectations are most tightly linked ..."). -> use the same wording
    in both places, e.g. "Do expectations or reasons point to an accuracy-based barrier?"

8.  Sec. 4.3 (p.19): code-style names in running text ("EVAlgorithm",
    "WithinPartDecisionNumber", "share[Payoff = 0]").
    -> use the notation of Eq. (1): "the EV coefficient", "EV x decision number",
    "EV x zero share".

9.  Figure 4 (p.29): axis labels are variable names (DontTrust, Responsibility4Choice,
    Reactance2Analysis, DislikeRelyingOn ...). -> plain-English labels ("Don't trust",
    "Wants responsibility for choice", "Dislikes being analyzed", ...).
    Caption: "Distribution of reasons indicated for delegating or not 10 additional
    hypothetical tasks" -> "Reasons for (not) delegating 10 additional hypothetical
    tasks"; delete the last caption sentence (91%/95%), which repeats the text.
[LEAVE AS IS]

10. Figure 4 placement (pp.28-29): the float interrupts a sentence ("...suggestive
    proxies for [figure] motives rather than as causal identification..."). -> re-anchor
    the float so the paragraph is unbroken.
[LEAVE AS IS]

11. Colloquial phrases (replace one-for-one):
    - "the first-contact gap remained alive" (p.34)                  -> "persisted"
    - "does not contribute too much to explaining" (p.33)           -> "contributes little to explaining"
    - "anything but huge characteristic differences" (p.22)         -> "anything but very large differences"
    - "the reason for why we did not pre-register" (fn. 9, p.32)    -> "the reason we did not preregister"

B. CONSISTENCY OF CONVENTIONS (the friction here is inconsistency, not the choice itself)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    but has "colouring" (p.15), "we analyse it" (p.23) and "re-analysing" (fn. 7, p.21).
    -> "coloring", "analyze", "reanalyzing" (keep "analysed" inside the verbatim
    participant items on pp.27 and 34).

    -> "November 13, 2025".
[Could we make it all British?]

13. Hyphenation: "preregistered" (about 50 times) vs. "pre-registered" / "pre-register"
    (pp.18, 32) and "pre-specify" / "pre-specified" (pp.13, 23).
    -> "preregistered", "prespecified" throughout.
[let's keep the hyphens in for both]

14. One term per concept: "never-delegators" (about 30 times) vs. "non-delegators"
    (p.3, p.37). -> "never-delegators" in both places.
    [CAREFUL: these are different concepts! Never-delegators should be the term for all instances where we are talking about those who do not delegate throughout all rounds 1-8; "non-delegators" should be used when referring to those who chose not to delegate in the hypothetical delegation choice at the end; not sure how often the latter are referred to, though]
    Small slips: "aversion/ appreciation" (p.12) -> "aversion/appreciation";
    heading 2.3 "Transparency, and interpretive cues" (p.9) -> delete the comma.


15. APA-7 statistics (the paper itself declares APA 7 via apacite):
    - no leading zero for p: "p = .041", "p < .001"
    - report degrees of freedom: "t(df) = -4.15", "chi2(11) = 13.17" (currently
      "t = -4.15" and "chi2 = 13.171, df = 11"); use two decimals consistently
    - Fig. 2 caption: "95%-confidence bands" -> "95% confidence intervals".
